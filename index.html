Q1 — E-R Diagram: Integrated Two-Wheeler Manufacturing Database Management System

1. Requirements Recap (from Set-B Case Study)

A two-wheeler manufacturing company makes Motorcycles, Scooters, Electric Scooters, Electric Motorcycles. It has multiple manufacturing plants, a network of suppliers and authorized dealers, and needs an integrated system covering: vehicle models, components, suppliers, manufacturing, inventory, employees, dealers, customers, sales, service and warranty — replacing today's separate, non-integrated systems.

Problems the design must solve:

  • Track vehicles from manufacturing → dealer → customer delivery
  • Reject duplicate vehicle keys and customer contact identifiers (UNIQUE constraints)
  • Track which components (and from which supplier/batch) are fitted in each vehicle → defect recall support
  • Supplier performance tracking (supply history, lead time)
  • Correct inventory of components at plants and dealers
  • Warranty claim tracking and service history
  • Production cost calculation (component cost via Bill of Materials)

The diagram abbreviates composite constraints: BATCH and SUPPLY_ORDER each reference SUPPLIES(supplier_id, component_id); VEHICLE_COMPONENT references BATCH(batch_no, component_id). The fitment serial uniqueness rule is UNIQUE(component_id, serial_no). Q2 lists these constraints explicitly.

6. Design decisions and scope

  1. Batch identity: batch_no is an internal globally unique lot code. It does not assume different suppliers coordinate their own batch numbering. BATCH is existence-dependent through SUPPLIES but has its own complete primary key.
  2. BOM versus original build: MODEL_COMPONENT records required quantities; VEHICLE_COMPONENT records individual physical parts, with separate fitment IDs for two tyres or other repeated components. Serials, when present, are assumed unique within a component type. The sample records demonstrate traceability rather than a complete build manifest for every vehicle. Subsequent replacements are described in service/claim records; this is not a full part-transfer history.
  3. Contact uniqueness: phone/email constraints reject repeated contact identifiers. They assume distinct customer/dealer contacts; records with different contacts may still describe the same person and require reconciliation.
  4. Inventory: plant/dealer quantities and reorder levels support stock reporting. Receipts, consumption and adjustments must update stock transactionally; the sample does not implement a stock-movement ledger.
  5. Supplier performance: each SUPPLY_ORDER stores its agreed lead time and actual delivery date. Later changes to the current SUPPLIES lead time do not alter historical on-time counts. Quality rating is a current value on SUPPLIES, not per-order quality history. An undelivered order is not counted as on-time; that alone does not classify it as overdue.
  6. Warranty and service: a vehicle can have several policies and service records; claims belong to a policy. An employee is optional on a service record because it can be unknown or removed.
  7. Sales: the model records one original sale per vehicle. Status and sale fields track the current manufacturing/dealer/customer stage. Sold requires dealer, customer, sale date and positive price; At-Dealer requires a dealer and no customer; plant stages have neither. A detailed shipping or resale history is outside this schema.
  8. Cost estimate: the BOM query calculates material cost using current component costs. It excludes labour, overhead and historical purchase-cost variation, so it is not a complete accounting production cost.

Q2 — Relational Schema (converted from the E-R Diagram)

Conversion rules applied

  • Strong entity → table with its primary key.
  • 1:N relationship → foreign key on the N side.
  • M:N relationship → separate relation (junction table) with the parent keys and relationship attributes. Use a surrogate key when the same pair can occur repeatedly, as with individual fitted parts.
  • BATCH and SERVICE_RECORD have their own complete keys and are strong entities, although their existence depends on parent records. BATCH uses a globally unique internal batch_no; its supplier/component pair is a foreign key to SUPPLIES.

Schema (underlined = primary key, # = foreign key)

Notation: EMP(emp_id, emp_name, ...) — attribute underlined as PK, foreign key prefixed FK:

  1. PLANT (plant_id, plant_name, location, annual_capacity)

  2. VEHICLE_MODEL (model_code, model_name, vehicle_type, engine_capacity_cc, fuel_type, transmission, seating_capacity, ex_showroom_price)

    • CHECK: vehicle_type ∈ {Motorcycle, Scooter, Electric Scooter, Electric Motorcycle}
    • Petrol models must be Motorcycle/Scooter with positive engine capacity; electric types must use Electric fuel and NULL engine capacity.

  3. COMPONENT (component_id, component_name, category, unit_cost, is_critical)

  4. SUPPLIER (supplier_id, supplier_name, address, supplier_type, rating)

  5. SUPPLIES (supplier_id, component_id, unit_price, lead_time_days, quality_rating)

    • FK: supplier_id → SUPPLIER, component_id → COMPONENT (M:N resolved)

  6. SUPPLY_ORDER (po_id, supplier_id, component_id, qty_ordered, unit_price, order_date, agreed_lead_time_days, delivery_date)

    • FK: supplier_id → SUPPLIER, component_id → COMPONENT, (supplier_id, component_id) → SUPPLIES
    • CHECK: unit_price and agreed_lead_time_days ≥ 0; delivery_date is NULL or ≥ order_date. The agreed lead time is stored on the order, independently of later changes to SUPPLIES.

  7. BATCH (batch_no, supplier_id, component_id, batch_qty, batch_date)

    • FK: (supplier_id, component_id) → SUPPLIES (batch_no is globally unique; batch tied to one supplier–component pair)

  8. MODEL_COMPONENT (BOM) (model_code, component_id, qty_per_vehicle)

    • FK: model_code → VEHICLE_MODEL, component_id → COMPONENT (M:N Uses)

  9. VEHICLE (vin, model_code, plant_id, mfg_date, color, status, dealer_id, customer_id, sale_date, sale_price)

    • FK: model_code → VEHICLE_MODEL, plant_id → PLANT, dealer_id → DEALER, customer_id → CUSTOMER
    • CHECK: status ∈ {In-Production, At-Plant, At-Dealer, Sold}

  10. VEHICLE_COMPONENT (as-built) (fitment_id, vin, component_id, batch_no, install_date, serial_no)

    • FK: vin → VEHICLE, component_id → COMPONENT, (batch_no, component_id) → BATCH(batch_no, component_id)

Key/Referential-Integrity Summary

  • Total tables: 18. The five M:N relationships become junction tables: SUPPLIES, MODEL_COMPONENT, VEHICLE_COMPONENT, PLANT_INVENTORY, and DEALER_INVENTORY.
  • Implemented relationships: 24 foreign-key constraints. Composite foreign keys count once each. Q1 Section 3 lists all 24 links and numbers them to match the Chen diagram; 18 is the table count, not the relationship count.
  • Primary keys: 18; Composite PKs: SUPPLIES, MODEL_COMPONENT, PLANT_INVENTORY, DEALER_INVENTORY. BATCH uses batch_no; VEHICLE_COMPONENT uses fitment_id.
  • Foreign keys: actions match Q3_two_wheeler_db.sql. History that must be kept (supply orders, batches, vehicles, dealers, and the component/batch side of fitment) uses ON DELETE RESTRICT. Rows that belong to a parent (supplies, inventories, service, warranty, claims, and the vehicle side of fitment) use ON DELETE CASCADE. service_record.emp_id uses ON DELETE SET NULL. A supply order and a batch must reference a real SUPPLIES pair. A fitted part must reference a batch of that same component.
  • UNIQUE constraints: CUSTOMER(phone), CUSTOMER(email), DEALER(phone), VEHICLE_COMPONENT(component_id, serial_no), BATCH(batch_no, component_id). Contact uniqueness assumes customers/dealers use distinct contact identifiers; it does not prove that records with different contacts represent different people.
  • Domains and checks: ENUM domains constrain statuses and types. CHECKs enforce fuel/type/engine agreement, positive sale prices, sale dates against status and manufacture date, delivery chronology, nonnegative order prices and lead times, quantities and ratings. MySQL 8.0.16+ is required. MySQL prohibits the configured foreign-key actions on columns used in CHECKs, so INSERT/UPDATE triggers enforce dealer/customer presence: Sold needs both, At-Dealer needs only a dealer, and a vehicle still at the plant needs neither.
  • Every problem from the case study maps to a construct:
    • Track vehicle manufacturing → delivery → VEHICLE(plant_id, dealer_id, customer_id, status)
    • Duplicates → UNIQUE constraints
    • Components used in a vehicle / defective component recall → VEHICLE_COMPONENT + BATCH
    • Supplier performance → SUPPLIES + SUPPLY_ORDER
    • Inventory accuracy → PLANT_INVENTORY / DEALER_INVENTORY
    • Warranty & service history → WARRANTY, WARRANTY_CLAIM, SERVICE_RECORD
    • Material cost estimate → SUM(MODEL_COMPONENT.qty_per_vehicle × COMPONENT.unit_cost); labour and overhead are outside this estimate.

Q3. MySQL implementation (10 marks)

Download the SQL script. It creates 18 tables and inserts records into every table. Requires MySQL 8.0.16 or later for enforced CHECK constraints.

The script recreates two_wheeler_db, replacing any existing database with that name. Run it in a disposable assignment database. PostgreSQL requires a separate port.

mysql -u root -p < Q3_two_wheeler_db.sql

Each physical fitted part has its own ID. The sample includes two unnumbered tyres of the same type on one vehicle. Orders preserve their agreed lead time, and checks enforce fuel/type consistency, positive sale prices and delivery chronology.

Defect recall: one row per affected VIN, even if two parts share a batch

SELECT DISTINCT vc.vin
FROM vehicle_component vc
WHERE vc.batch_no = 'BAT3K-A117';

Production (material) cost of each model from the Bill of Materials

SELECT mc.model_code,
       SUM(mc.qty_per_vehicle * c.unit_cost) AS material_cost
FROM model_component mc
JOIN component c ON c.component_id = mc.component_id
GROUP BY mc.model_code
ORDER BY material_cost DESC;

Supplier performance: on-time delivery count per supplier

SELECT s.supplier_id, s.supplier_name,
       COUNT(*) AS total_orders,
       SUM(CASE WHEN so.delivery_date IS NOT NULL
                 AND DATEDIFF(so.delivery_date, so.order_date) <= so.agreed_lead_time_days
                THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS on_time_orders
FROM supplier s
JOIN supply_order so ON so.supplier_id = s.supplier_id
GROUP BY s.supplier_id, s.supplier_name;

Components below reorder level at any plant

SELECT p.plant_name, c.component_name, pi.qty_on_hand, pi.reorder_level
FROM plant_inventory pi
JOIN plant p ON p.plant_id = pi.plant_id
JOIN component c ON c.component_id = pi.component_id
WHERE pi.qty_on_hand < pi.reorder_level;

Full service history of a vehicle with mechanic details

SELECT v.vin, m.model_name, sr.service_date, sr.service_type, sr.odometer_km, sr.cost, e.emp_name
FROM vehicle v
JOIN vehicle_model m ON m.model_code = v.model_code
JOIN service_record sr ON sr.vin = v.vin
LEFT JOIN employee e ON e.emp_id = sr.emp_id
WHERE v.vin = 'MAT447000A1000001'
ORDER BY sr.service_date;

Sales report: vehicles sold with customer, dealer and price

SELECT c.customer_name, d.dealer_name, m.model_name, v.vin, v.sale_date, v.sale_price
FROM vehicle v
JOIN customer c ON c.customer_id = v.customer_id
JOIN dealer d   ON d.dealer_id   = v.dealer_id
JOIN vehicle_model m ON m.model_code = v.model_code
WHERE v.status = 'Sold'
ORDER BY v.sale_date;

The cost query estimates materials only. Labour, overhead and historical procurement costs are outside its scope. An undelivered order is not counted as on-time; it is not necessarily overdue.

Q4 — NoSQL vs RDBMS for the Two-Wheeler Manufacturing System

NoSQL includes different database families. The following ten points compare specific capabilities with MySQL/PostgreSQL; they are not universal properties of every NoSQL database.

Potential advantages

  1. Flexible product attributes. A MongoDB document can store battery chemistry on an electric model and engine capacity on a petrol model. This suits varying specifications. Relational systems can also use subtype tables or JSON columns, so flexibility is not exclusive to NoSQL.
  2. Distributed workloads. Cassandra distributes data across nodes, which can suit vehicle telemetry from many plants. Partition keys and access patterns must be designed carefully. Relational systems can also scale through partitioning, replication and distributed implementations; NoSQL is not automatically cheaper or faster.
  3. Availability choices. Cassandra offers tunable consistency for reads and writes. Less critical sensor readings can favour availability, while stock decisions need stronger coordination. This is a workload-specific tradeoff, not a reason to weaken every transaction. Cassandra architecture
  4. Related data in one document. A model and its BOM can be embedded together for a single read. Updating one document is atomic in MongoDB. Duplicating component descriptions across models, however, creates extra update work. MongoDB transactions
  5. Variable service and claim details. Documents can hold different inspection results, diagnostic fields and attachment metadata without many optional columns. Large photos and invoices can remain in file/object storage with references; embedding arbitrary files is not an inherent NoSQL advantage.

Limitations and design tradeoffs

  1. Transactions depend on the product and deployment. MongoDB supports multi-document ACID transactions on replica sets and sharded clusters, with overhead compared with single-document writes. A sale that changes several documents still needs an explicit transaction, just as several SQL updates do. It is incorrect to say NoSQL cannot provide ACID. MongoDB transactions
  2. Uniqueness and referential integrity are different. MongoDB unique indexes can reject duplicate VINs or phone numbers, subject to sharding/index restrictions. Cross-collection references do not have SQL-style foreign-key enforcement, so ensuring every referenced model exists requires additional application/database design. Unique indexes
  3. Reports depend on the data model. MongoDB's $lookup performs joins inside an aggregation pipeline, and graph databases are designed around relationships. Complex supplier–vehicle–service reports may nevertheless need careful indexing or denormalization; they do not necessarily require application-side joins. MongoDB $lookup
  4. Schema validation is available but must be configured. MongoDB validators can require fields and constrain types and values, preventing inconsistent date names or textual prices. A flexible collection without suitable validation can drift; that is not the same as having no enforcement capability. Schema validation
  5. Consistency must match each operation. Cassandra's consistency level and replication design affect what reads observe. Stale stock is a risk under weak consistency choices, not an unavoidable property of NoSQL. Relational read replicas may also lag. Selling a specific vehicle requires an atomic availability check/update, with suitable consistency and transaction settings. Cassandra architecture

Recommendation for this case

Use an RDBMS for the integrated core because its foreign keys, constraints, joins and transactions fit the highly related manufacturing, sales and service records. Consider a document or distributed store for variable diagnostics or high-volume telemetry when workload evidence justifies it. The choice depends on required guarantees and measured workload, not a blanket SQL/NoSQL ranking.
